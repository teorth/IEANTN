How to read it

The colour of a box is the kind of evidence behind the step from its assumptions to its claim, and only green was checked by Lean here. Everything else is a leaf of the trust graph — something the network takes on faith, however reasonably — and the point of drawing it is that you can see exactly which.

Solid green
A verification receipt that still stands.
Pale green
Stale: only the environment has moved on, so re-running is bookkeeping.
Grey-green
Drifted: the statement itself changed since verification, so the checked implication no longer reaches what the node claims. Ranked below a bare assertion for that reason.
Dashed border
The inputs are known and written down, but at least one is not the sort of thing an arrow can carry — an algorithm, a data set, a paper nobody has made a node of yet.
Dotted border
Nobody has yet worked out what the claim assumes.
Hexagon
A bridge: the step itself, proved in Lean and recompiled on every push. Drawn as a box rather than an arrow because a bridge may have several premises at once, which an arrow cannot say.

Every box links to its node's page, which carries the Lean spelling of the claim, its docstring, and everything recorded about why it should be believed.
